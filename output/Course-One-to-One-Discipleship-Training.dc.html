C. Preparation for Quiet Time

1. Your Mind

You are about to have fellowship with God, the Almighty Creator.

Prepare your mind with expectation and fear. In this case, the fear is

related to reverence to God. It is a combination of love and respect for an

awesome God.

2. The Bible and QT Notebook

Open your Bible to the passage on which you will meditate for the day's

QT. We recommend you to have QT systematically. Once you start a

book in the Bible for your QT, continue the book until you finish it.

Normally one paragraph is appropriate for a day's QT. If it is too

short, you can continue to the next paragraph. If it is too long, you can

stop in the middle of the paragraph. Be flexible on the amount for a day's

QT. Sometimes the Holy Spirit teaches enough lessons from one verse. If

that is the case, stop there and continue with the remaining verses on the

next day.

Be ready to write down what the Holy Spirit impresses on you in

your QT notebook. Use the format provided at the end of this lesson for

recording your insights. Do not use any secondary material for your QT.

You need to have direct fellowship with the Holy Spirit through his word.

3. Place and Time

The place for your QT is very important. You want to have a personal

fellowship with the Lord, and you do not want to be disturbed. Find a

place where you can spend twenty to thirty minutes with God without any

disturbance.

Time often plays a crucial role in having consistent QT. Usually

early morning is the most appropriate time for QT because you can start

a new day with his guidance. However the time for QT is flexible

depending on one’s situation. The important thing is to make sure the time

for QT is not sacrificed because of other urgent matters in your life and

ministry.

D. Suggested Method for Quiet Time

The purpose of QT is not to understand the Scriptures thoroughly but to

have God speak to you through his word. You can start QT using the

following steps:

1. Praise God

Start your QT with praise. To praise God means to commend him for all

that he has done. Please read Psalm 59:17 for an example of praising God.

You can praise God in prayer, while singing songs at church, while

listening to praise music, or while meditating on the Psalms, such as

Psalm 98. Praise prepares the heart to go before God.

2. Depend on the Holy Spirit

The Holy Spirit is the author of the Bible, which was written through his

inspiration. Therefore he illumines us to understand the Scriptures.

3. Find Truths by Meditating on the Word

God wants to reveal himself to us through the Scriptures (giving comfort,

hope, warning, direction, etc.). Therefore we need to meditate on the

words he gives us each day. During your QT, read the given text several

times. Read his words with humility before God and the respect for his

authority. Also, the more we meditate upon God’s word, the more our

minds become focused on his eternal truths.

Meditate on the Scripture and find his eternal truths like the

following:

1) Sins to confess

2) Promises to keep

4) Actions to take

5) Knowledge on God, Jesus Christ, or the Holy Spirit

Remember an English word, SPEAK. Allow the Holy Spirit to SPEAK

to you through his word. The Holy Spirit sometimes may illumine several

truths from one verse, and sometimes none. Remember that you do not

have to understand everything in the passage, but you need to listen to

what the Holy Spirit is speaking to you through the passage at that

moment. You may have to read the same verse several times over in order

The Bible is a compilation of books written by around forty authors in a

period spanning 1,500 years. The Old Testament is the word that Moses

and the prophets received from God. It has been handed down orally or

in writing through history. The Pentateuch was established as the

authorized version in the fifth century BC; the books of the prophets and

the books of literature in the second century BC. The Old Testament is

composed of thirty-nine books and is written mostly in Hebrew.

The New Testament is a compilation of books and letters written by the

disciples who were inspired by God. Recognition of a formal canon of the

New Testament did not occur until long after the time of its writing. The

collection and recognition of all the New Testament books was a gradual

process. During the first century of the church (from Pentecost to about

AD 140), there was only a very slow distribution and recognition of the

writings that later were brought together and recognized as inspired. After

AD 140 a more rapid recognition of the canonical books occurred. By the

end of the second century almost all of the books were known and

recognized as authoritative. Finally, by the end of the fourth century the

official canon of the New Testament was recognized and fixed.

Why did it take so long for the authority of all the New Testament

books to be acknowledged by the church? First, the New Testament

church already had a canon of Scripture—the Old Testament—which was

considered all that was needed in the church ―for teaching, for reproof,

for correction, and for training in righteousness (2 Timothy 3:16).

Second, as long as the apostles were alive, there was little felt need

for an authoritative body of writings. The early church already had the

Old Testament and the living apostles to teach them (Acts 2:42). There

was no urgency to copy and distribute the New Testament books so that

they could be widely known and acknowledged.

Third, the recipients of the New Testament books were spread all

across the Mediterranean basin. It is likely that churches would share with

other churches any authoritative books that they received from the

apostles. But because of poor communication and the slow production of

books, no single church would always know about all of the apostolic

writings in existence. Churches would receive various portions of the

New Testament without knowing what portions other churches possessed.

The New Testament is composed of twenty-seven books and was, in the

original manuscripts, written entirely in Greek.

The Bible’s purpose is not only to educate us or give us mere knowledge,

but it is also to teach us knowledge about the saving grace of God and

how to walk with God in our daily lives.

Prayer is the natural overflow of meaningful time in Scripture. Prayer to

God completes the fellowship element in your relationship with him.

Respond back to God in prayer after he speaks to you through his word.

In this manner, you share your heart with the One who longs for your

companionship.

Prayer is how the power of God is unleashed. Personal battles and

battles for others are won in prayer, and the cause of Christ is thus

furthered. Through prayer, you have the power and the privilege to be

used by God to help change lives. We do not avail ourselves of this power

primarily because of our unbelief. The disciples of the first century knew

how to pray and claimed in faith the power of our omnipotent God to help

change the course of history. We serve the same powerful God today and

we too are able to call upon him in prayer.

A. What Is Prayer?

Prayer is simply communicating with God. It is a dialogue between two

who love each other—God and human. As children of God, we are invited

to draw near to the throne of grace with confidence (Hebrews 4:16).

To know exactly what prayer is, we need to know about the widely

held misconceptions regarding prayer.

1. Prayer is not meditation by oneself. Prayer is offered to the

living God.

2. Prayer is different from requesting to other gods in other

religions. Prayer can only be offered in his name by those who

believe in Christ (John 16:24).

3. Prayer is not just about making requests. The main purpose of

prayer is to deepen our relationship with God.

4. Prayer is not just offered in public but starts in a private room

(Matthew 6:6).

5. Prayer is not repeating meaningless religious words. Prayer is

the deliverance of specific intentions.

D. Why Are We to Pray?

1. To Communicate with God

We have to pray to maintain a loving relationship. God waits for us to

come to him through prayer. The fact that God wants to have fellowship

with us is an incredible fact.

2. To Glorify God (John 14:13)

We glorify God when we remember who he is and what he has done for

us during prayer.

Just as a child needs food to grow physically, we need food to grow

spiritually. Prayer which involves our talking to God and God talking to

us through his word is our major source of spiritual food. We can miss a

day without apparent ill effects; however if we continue this practice, we

shall become undernourished, losing the strength to live a victorious life

and to maintain boldness for a vital witness for Christ.

Prayer is the divinely ordained means whereby God moves in the

accomplishment of his mighty operations on this earth. The Bible records

that weather was changed and people were released from prison as a result

of prayer. God is still in the business of answering prayer. Prayer is the

means of becoming a fruitful witness for Christ.

B. The Fellowship of Christians

1. Be Members of One Another

So we, though many, are one body in Christ, and individually

members one of another. (Romans 12:5)

If it is true that everyone is equal under the cross, every Christian has the

privilege of becoming an integral member of the family of God. In the

family of God, we must be willing to learn from one another and to

encourage one another. The fellowship of the saints can fully develop

only when Christians are diligent in building one another up. We cannot

participate in this fellowship while isolating ourselves from the

community of saints. God desires that all Christians become active

members of God's family by joining a local church. The New Testament

states that Christians are “members one of another.” Paul used an

illustration to get this point across—the human body.

2. Be United in Christ

There is one body and one Spirit—just as you were called to the

one hope that belongs to your call— (Ephesians 4:4)

The human body is not made up of separate and individual members.

Rather, the body exists as a unified whole of many parts that are members

of one body. Similarly, the church is not an association of individual

Christians but is the body of Christ, consisting of people who are

members of one another from the inception of their Christian lives.

In its broadest sense, the body of Christ is the historical and global

community of the saints. The Bible frequently teaches about the body of

Christ in the context of a specific local church community. Hence

Christians must participate in the life of a local church and joyfully take

on their respective roles as members of the body.

Satan’s strategy involves destroying unity among Christians. He

is the author of confusion, insensitivity, and false doctrines. In the Bible,

we discover the power whereby Satan's strategy can be defeated.

Gifts are essential to do the ministry of God. But for that gift to bear fruit,

training is needed. It is wrong for a church to give someone an important

role without training that person first. Those who desire to serve God

should make necessary preparations. Training that is in line with the gift

becomes an appropriate preparation for the church ministry.

Two inherent problems that our churches have are (1) there are not

many pastors who want to train the believers and (2) there are few

believers who desire to receive training. Some people think training is

difficult and still others think that it is unnecessary. However the training

for believers is imperative for a church to become a fully functioning,

spiritually effective church.
