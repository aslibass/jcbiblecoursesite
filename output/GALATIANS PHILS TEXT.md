In this course you will study Paul's letter to the Galatians. You will look
at its background and examine the vital theological issues dealt in the
early church. You will become better acquainted with the life, character,

Foreword
“The righteous shall live by faith” (***Galatians 3:11***). The emphasis Martin
Luther placed on this truth in the sixteenth century resulted in the
Protestant Reformation. This doctrine of justification by faith brought
new life into the Church and became one of the central teachings of the
evangelical churches. An understanding of this all-important biblical
concept will result in a deeper appreciation of the grace of God as it has
come to us in the Lord Jesus Christ. Our study will take us through the
book of Galatians which deals in detail with this fundamental doctrine.
From its earliest days the Christian Church has raised questions
concerning the relationship between law and grace. What was the purpose
of the giving of the law in the Old Testament? Were godly people in Old
Testament times saved by obeying the law? Does the plan of salvation in
the New Testament differ from that in the Old Testament? Is a person
saved by faith alone? Or is it a matter of faith plus work? Does the New
Testament really teach that salvation must come through Jesus Christ
alone? If a person is saved by faith alone does it make no difference what
kind of life he lives? Why was it necessary for the Son of God to die on
the cross in order to provide salvation for mankind? Paul deals with all
these crucial questions in Galatians.
Matters of theology must always be examined in the light of the
Scriptures. It is with this in mind that we approach our study of Paul's
letter to the Galatians. We will do it reverently because these are part of
God's Word. We will do it prayerfully because we need the guidance of
the Holy Spirit who inspired it through the apostle. We will do it carefully
and analytically because we need to love the Lord our God not only with
our hearts but also with our minds (***Matthew 22:37***). We will do it
gratefully because we have already experienced God's grace and are now
given an opportunity to understand it better.
Our aim for this study goes beyond understanding the doctrines
in Galatians. We also have a practical aim. Paul intentionally wrote this
letter with both doctrinal and practical divisions. He wants his audience
to know that the righteousness of God received by faith is related to
conduct. Paul was more than a theologian; he related what he believed
ix
x • Galatians
and taught about God to real life. For him doctrine and practice were
inseparable.
The faith that obtains the righteousness of God is to issue forth in
living faith. The initial act of faith is to give birth to a living faith. The
word living here is not only living which is the opposite of dead but also
faith which is at work in life. It is a faith for living day by day—not just
the single act which brings right standing with God but also the
continuous trust in and response to God.
Perhaps you have heard the saying, “the only gospel some people
ever read is your life.” As the key verse above states, in the gospel the
righteousness of God is revealed by faith. It also means that the
righteousness of God is revealed in you as you live by faith from day to
day. This is our practical aim for you in this study.

• Apostle − one who is sent, missionary. Apostle comes from the

King.

the universe; system of religious beliefs. Saul's theological training

nature of God and gives us the history of his relations to man and the
universe up until the coming of Christ. Paul's epistles are a clear
presentation of Christian theology.

> **Question 17: What is the standard by which we must measure all religious**

The principle in verses 8-9 deals with two truths: 1) The finality of the
gospel, 2) The relationship of the messenger to his message.
   **a. Finality of the Gospel**
First, the gospel is a final revelation. There will be no more revelations
that will add to the message of God's redeeming work. It is not to be
changed or replaced by any newer revelation. The gospel of the grace of
Christ that God sent Paul out to preach—the good news of salvation by
faith in Christ—is the true and everlasting gospel. There can be no true
revelation of a new way to get right with God, because faith in Christ is
the only way.
   **b. Relationship of Messenger to Message**
Second, the gospel becomes the basis for evaluating not only the message
but also the messenger. No matter what credentials the messenger may
have, these do not commend the message, but rather the message
commends the messenger.
