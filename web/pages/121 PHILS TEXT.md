Introduction
Jesus commanded his disciples, “Go therefore and make disciples of all
nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of
the Holy Spirit, teaching them to observe all that I have commanded you.
And behold, I am with you always, to the end of the age.” (Matthew
28:19-20) The command of our Lord Jesus Christ is very clear; make
disciples. Then he makes clear the process of making disciples: to go, to
baptize (evangelize), and to teach them to observe all that he has
commanded us. And then he encourages us with the promise to be with
us in the task…even to the end of the age.
Paul, the apostle, in a letter to Timothy makes clear a principle that
enables Jesus’ command to be fulfilled through all who know him…the
principle of multiplication. “and what you have heard me in the presence
of many witnesses entrust to faithful men who will be able to teach others
also.” (***2 Timothy 2:2***)
One-to-One Discipleship Training was designed to encourage this
principle of multiplication and to help you fulfill the command of our
Lord to make disciples! Here are some practical steps:
 Study this material with a facilitator for your own
understanding and spiritual growth.
 Take Evangelism and Discipleship 2 offered by BEE to learn
how to use this material to make your own disciples.
 Think and pray about someone who can benefit from
studying this course.
v
 Plan to meet with this person on a regular basis (at least once
a week). Study and discuss each lesson together with the
goal of good understanding and personal application.
The joys will be many as you help this person build a confident and
mature relationship with Jesus and to enable this person to help others
also.
# DESCRIPTION OF THE COURSE
This course provides three systematic approaches to fulfill this command.
Unit 1 is designed to help new believers or unbelievers understand who
Jesus is or believe in him by introducing him to them. If you do not know
who Jesus is, or if you are not sure of your salvation, study this unit
carefully with your facilitator. We recommend you studying this unit even
if you already accepted Jesus as your Savior and Lord. The more
knowledge you have of the truth of the gospel the easier it will be to share
this truth with unbelievers.
Unit 2 introduces the importance of a devotional Bible study or
Quiet Time. A Christian cannot grow spiritually without interacting with
the word of God. God’s word is spiritual nourishment for the believer.
Study this unit and make Quiet Time a part of your life. You will
experience spiritual growth through reading, meditating, and applying the
word of God in your daily Quiet Time. Unit 3 deals with topics essential
to Christian living that will equip you in areas important for spiritual
maturity.
Remember that the knowledge you learn from this course is not
enough to change your life. Your daily trusting in Christ, your
commitment, and your practice of that knowledge will help make the
change (***Hebrews 5:12***-14). Whatever you learn start practicing
immediately. It is our desire that you grow spiritually and bear fruit that
you might produce a hundredfold as a disciple of Christ (***Mark 4:1***-9).

Each lesson is divided into the following parts: Title, Lesson Introduction,
Lesson Outline, Lesson Objectives, Assignments, and Lesson
Development. The title, introduction, outline, and objectives provide a
preview of the lesson. The lesson assignments describe specific tasks

Please give careful attention to every part of the lessons. Read all
comments and answer to all questions. These will fix your attention once
more on the main points of the lesson. This procedure is designed to make

Student
Instructions
In each lesson there are assignments to be fulfilled before you study the
lesson with your facilitator in order to satisfactorily complete the lesson.
The following gives a list of differing types of assignments with a general
description:
Lesson preparation. The purpose of this assignment is to use your
time with facilitator effectively as well as to make you understand the
content of the lesson before you study it with your facilitator. You and
your facilitator are committing valuable time for this training. It is our
desire that both of you use this time most efficiently. The most important
purpose of this training is not transferring the knowledge in this workbook.
As Jesus spent his time with his disciples so that they could learn from
his teaching and life, you need to spend quality time with your facilitator
together through this workbook to grow as Jesus’ disciple. When you
prepare for the lesson by answering all the questions in the lesson, you
are ready to spend more meaningful time with your facilitator beyond
acquiring knowledge. You must look for the given passages to answer
most questions. We recommend you writing down the passages you look
for on a separate notebook. By doing so, not only will you prepare for the
training as a student but also as a facilitator in the future.
Scripture memory. The purpose of this assignment is to build into
your heart and mind Scriptures that will strengthen you in your daily walk
with Jesus. In each lesson you will be asked to memorize verses of
Scripture related to the content of the lesson. Choose a translation of the
ix
Bible you are reading consistently.
Take sermon notes from a worship service. The purpose of this
assignment is to encourage you toward regular worship with other
believers and attentiveness to the message given each week. As you
attend a weekly worship service, bring paper and pen to write down what
you learn from the sermon given by the pastor.
Daily Bible reading. The purpose of this assignment is to
encourage in your life a habit of daily Bible reading. Starting with Unit
III you are asked to read a certain number of chapters from the Bible each
week.
Daily Quiet Time (QT). The purpose of this assignment is to help
you develop a daily time alone with the Lord and his word. Unit II will
give you practical helps to make this a meaningful source of daily
strengthening in the Lord and your walk with him. A part of this
assignment will be to record what you are learning in a Quite Time
notebook. You will need a separate notebook for this purpose.
Memorize the names of all the books of the Bible. The purpose of
this assignment is to make you very familiar with the whole Bible and to
more easily know where to find passages of Scripture.

**1. Your Mind**
You are about to have fellowship with God, the Almighty Creator.

awesome God.
**2. The Bible and QT Notebook**
Open your Bible to the passage on which you will meditate for the day's

The place for your QT is very important. You want to have a personal
fellowship with the Lord, and you do not want to be disturbed. Find a
place where you can spend twenty to thirty minutes with God without any
disturbance.

early morning is the most appropriate time for QT because you can start
a new day with his guidance. However the time for QT is flexible
depending on one’s situation. The important thing is to make sure the time

Objective 1: Describe the brief history of the formation of the Bible
51
52 ● One-to-One Discipleship Training
The Bible is a compilation of books written by around forty authors in a
period spanning 1,500 years. The Old Testament is the word that Moses
and the prophets received from God. It has been handed down orally or
in writing through history. The Pentateuch was established as the
authorized version in the fifth century BC; the books of the prophets and
the books of literature in the second century BC. The Old Testament is
composed of thirty-nine books and is written mostly in Hebrew.
The New Testament is a compilation of books and letters written by the
disciples who were inspired by God. Recognition of a formal canon of the
New Testament did not occur until long after the time of its writing. The
collection and recognition of all the New Testament books was a gradual
process. During the first century of the church (from Pentecost to about
AD 140), there was only a very slow distribution and recognition of the
writings that later were brought together and recognized as inspired. After
AD 140 a more rapid recognition of the canonical books occurred. By the
end of the second century almost all of the books were known and
recognized as authoritative. Finally, by the end of the fourth century the
official canon of the New Testament was recognized and fixed.
Why did it take so long for the authority of all the New Testament
books to be acknowledged by the church? First, the New Testament
church already had a canon of Scripture—the Old Testament—which was
considered all that was needed in the church ―for teaching, for reproof,
for correction, and for training in righteousness (***2 Timothy 3:16***).
Second, as long as the apostles were alive, there was little felt need
for an authoritative body of writings. The early church already had the
Old Testament and the living apostles to teach them (Acts 2:42). There
was no urgency to copy and distribute the New Testament books so that
they could be widely known and acknowledged.
Third, the recipients of the New Testament books were spread all
across the Mediterranean basin. It is likely that churches would share with
other churches any authoritative books that they received from the
apostles. But because of poor communication and the slow production of
books, no single church would always know about all of the apostolic
writings in existence. Churches would receive various portions of the
New Testament without knowing what portions other churches possessed.

#### D. Biblical View of Training

Objective 4: Indicate what training is necessary to be a good worker
for the Lord
Gifts are essential to do the ministry of God. But for that gift to bear fruit,
training is needed. It is wrong for a church to give someone an important
role without training that person first. Those who desire to serve God
should make necessary preparations. Training that is in line with the gift
becomes an appropriate preparation for the church ministry.
Two inherent problems that our churches have are (1) there are not
many pastors who want to train the believers and (2) there are few
believers who desire to receive training. Some people think training is
difficult and still others think that it is unnecessary. However the training
for believers is imperative for a church to become a fully functioning,
spiritually effective church.

#### E. Biblical View of the World
Objective 5: Find what you can do in this world, as a disciple of Jesus,
to fulfill his commission
For trained believers to minister, they need to understand their field of
ministry. Many Christians limit the scope of ministry to inside the church.
But the field of our ministry is this world. The world in which we live and
work is where we should minister. Many believers limit their field to the
region or country they belong to. But our scope of ministry is to the end
of the earth (Acts 1:8). By broadening our vision of the world, it helps us
to have a greater sense of God’s heart for the whole world.
